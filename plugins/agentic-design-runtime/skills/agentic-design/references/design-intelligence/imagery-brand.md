# Imagery, Graphics, and Brand Systems

Use this reference when choosing assets, crops, graphic language, Brand Kits, or campaign rules.

## Give every image a job

An image should show a product, establish emotion, provide evidence, identify a person or place, explain a process, create a relevant metaphor, establish context, or carry a defined Brand texture.

Do not add imagery merely because a layout feels empty. Prefer a specific narrative relationship to a generic category symbol.

## Evaluate source quality and rights

Check effective resolution, focus, compression, edges, lighting, color depth, crop flexibility, embedded text and logos, anatomy and object structure, provenance, and permitted use.

Never present generated people, events, evidence, product features, testimonials, certifications, or documentary scenes as factual. Verify cultural clothing, uniforms, symbols, and context when they matter.

## Crop with intent

A crop can intensify emotion, remove distraction, create negative space, align a subject to the grid, change perceived motion, or establish scale.

Protect eyes, faces, joints, hands, products, labels, horizons, gaze space, and relevant context. Avoid awkward anatomy or product crops unless the framing is deliberately decisive.

For composites, match perspective, horizon, light direction, shadow softness, temperature, scale, sharpness, depth of field, and contact shadows.

## Use graphics semantically

Shapes, rules, icons, patterns, and textures should support grouping, emphasis, direction, containment, annotation, data encoding, depth, or Brand recognition.

Use a coherent icon system and a limited shape language. Random rounded cards, floating blobs, and decorative gradients are not neutral defaults. Remove elements whose absence improves clarity without harming the concept or Brand.

## Treat a Brand as a controlled decision space

Separate:

- invariants: approved logos, type families, core palette, voice, legal text, signature behavior, and accessibility commitments;
- controlled variables: composition, crop, accent selection, scale, density, motif placement, background value, and texture intensity;
- prohibitions: unapproved assets, effects, colors, fonts, logo treatments, cross-brand motifs, or factual alterations.

Use exact immutable Brand Kit revisions, resource maps, and live bindings. Never follow a newer kit revision implicitly.

## Build a composition grammar

A useful Brand grammar describes observable behavior, for example:

```text
Primary copy is left aligned.
Images occupy 45% to 75% of the canvas.
One edge may break the grid deliberately.
Metadata uses a repeated compact label treatment.
Accent color appears once unless it carries data meaning.
The logo remains in a quiet attribution region.
```

Translate adjectives into behaviors. “Premium” might mean controlled space, precise alignment, limited roles, high-quality imagery, and restrained detail. “Energetic” might mean active crop, diagonal motion, scale contrast, and one saturated accent. These are hypotheses until Brand evidence or the owner confirms them.

## Preserve recognition without sameness

Campaign consistency can come from typography, color-role behavior, image treatment, spacing rhythm, logo placement family, recurring motifs, and compositional attitude. Vary content-led structure within that grammar so the system remains recognizable but not templated.

## Retrieve references structurally

Prefer references with a similar communication problem, content density, image-copy relationship, or format. Extract principles without copying distinctive artwork, competitor trade dress, or an unrelated Brand system.

Record approved and rejected examples with specific reasons: hierarchy, tone, crop, density, copy fidelity, Brand drift, or production failure. “The client did not like it” is not reusable guidance.

## Handle co-branding deliberately

Determine hierarchy from the actual agreement or brief. Normalize optical size, preserve each logo's clear space, avoid unauthorized recoloring, and distinguish primary ownership from endorsement. Equal contractual status does not require identical bounding-box dimensions.

## Brand and imagery gate

- The image has a specific communication function.
- Crop, resolution, perspective, and lighting are credible.
- Generated imagery contains no factual or structural defect.
- Rights and provenance are known where required.
- Shapes, icons, textures, and effects use one coherent language.
- The exact Brand Kit and logo assets are correct.
- Composition follows the Brand grammar with controlled variation.
- No motif, typeface, or treatment leaked from another project.
