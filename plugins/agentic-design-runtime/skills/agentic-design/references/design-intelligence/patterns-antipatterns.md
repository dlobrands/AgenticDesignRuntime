# Structural Patterns and Anti-Patterns

Use patterns as starting hypotheses, not finished templates. Choose from the content hierarchy, image availability, copy length, context, Brand grammar, and adaptation needs.

## Useful structural patterns

### Anchored hero

A dominant subject or product occupies most of the canvas while a compact copy group anchors to an edge or corner. Strong for launches, portraits, statements, and simple ads. Protect the subject and verify that text has a stable field.

### Editorial split

Image and copy occupy distinct but aligned fields. Strong for moderate copy and reliable legibility. Avoid a generic two-column result by making proportion, crop, edge behavior, or typography specific to the concept.

### Statement field

Typography is the primary visual; imagery is absent or subordinate. Strong for thought leadership and announcements. Line breaks, font choice, hierarchy, and optical spacing must carry the design.

### Framed window

Content sits within a deliberate field while a subject may break the frame and metadata occupies the outer area. Strong for campaign systems. The frame must have a Brand, grouping, or depth function rather than becoming another arbitrary card.

### Asymmetric editorial grid

Unequal spans on a six- or twelve-column grid create rhythm, with one intentional break. Strong for culturally or editorially expressive work. Declare the stable axes so offsets do not become accidents.

### Product spotlight

An isolated product is primary, followed by one benefit and a quiet proof or feature group. Ground the object through scale, contact, shadow, perspective, or context so it does not float meaninglessly.

### Modular information grid

Repeated modules organize categories, services, schedules, or comparisons. Create hierarchy across modules and avoid giving every item an equal card.

### Image-led negative space

Select or crop imagery to create a natural quiet field for copy. Strong for restrained emotional campaigns. Verify the weakest contrast point and crop stability.

### Diagonal energy

One directional crop, rule, or typographic axis creates motion while secondary groups follow or counterbalance it. Use one dominant directional family; too many angles produce noise.

### Layered collage

Several images or cutouts overlap while one subject remains dominant. Strong for culture and entertainment. Control z-order, saliency, edge treatment, lighting, and the reason for each overlap.

### Data-led hero

One truthful number or insight is primary, with a concise explanation, unit, and source. Avoid decorative chart forms that distort the relationship.

### Sequential narrative

Content progresses through steps or panels with recurring anchors and a clear entry and exit. Each frame must advance the narrative and remain understandable in sequence.

## Variation protocol

To prevent template sameness, vary several controlled dimensions such as proportion, focal position, crop, grid span, alignment axis, text-block shape, density, and layering while preserving the communication logic and Brand signature.

## Common AI anti-patterns

### Style before hierarchy

Symptom: gradients, glow, glass, or a fashionable font appear before message priority. Correction: return to brief, grayscale masses, and reading order.

### Center everything

Symptom: every group shares one center axis despite copy and image structure. Correction: choose alignment from reading behavior and meaningful geometry.

### Equal emphasis

Symptom: headline, CTA, date, logo, and labels all compete. Correction: restore one dominant P0 relationship and subordinate the rest.

### Arbitrary spacing or even distribution

Symptom: unrelated gaps vary randomly or every object is spaced evenly. Correction: use a small token set and semantic grouping.

### Edge hugging and accidental tangencies

Symptom: elements almost touch the canvas, frames, faces, or each other. Correction: separate, align, or overlap decisively.

### Image as wallpaper

Symptom: a full-bleed image is used without subject, crop, saliency, or text-zone analysis. Correction: map semantic space and design around it.

### White text plus shadow as the universal fix

Symptom: the same crude treatment appears over every photo. Correction: move, crop, recolor, add a coherent underlay, adjust locally, or separate fields.

### Decorative container spam

Symptom: every label becomes a rounded card. Correction: keep containers only where they group, separate, emphasize, or express Brand logic.

### Random gradients, blobs, or floating shapes

Symptom: empty space is filled with generic “AI” decoration. Correction: remove it or name its communication, movement, or Brand function.

### Too many typefaces or type effects

Symptom: expression substitutes for hierarchy. Correction: establish scale, grouping, alignment, and plain-type contrast first.

### Poor line breaks and shrink-to-fit

Symptom: phrases break arbitrarily or all copy becomes too small. Correction: adjust width, hierarchy, optional content, sequencing, or structure.

### Generic stock symbolism

Symptom: handshake for partnership, lightbulb for ideas, or network nodes for AI. Correction: choose a specific narrative tied to the proposition.

### Uniform scaling across ratios

Symptom: a portrait design is squeezed into square or landscape. Correction: preserve hierarchy while recomposing crop, groups, and line breaks.

### Blind code confidence and one-shot approval

Symptom: a valid scene is called final before pixel inspection or comparison. Correction: render, inspect, localize, revise, and rerender.

### Global rebuild for a local defect

Symptom: one line break or margin issue causes a new composition. Correction: preserve successful relationships and change the smallest affected region.

### Brand adjective literalism or cross-brand contamination

Symptom: “luxury” automatically becomes black and gold, or another client's motifs leak in. Correction: use exact Brand evidence and translate tone into observable behavior.

### Passing score despite a failed gate

Symptom: a high self-rating hides wrong copy, missing content, or unsupported production claims. Correction: deterministic and factual gates remain absolute; heuristic review never authorizes release.
