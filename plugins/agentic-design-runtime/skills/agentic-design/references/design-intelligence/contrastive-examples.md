# Contrastive Design Examples

Use these examples to isolate causal relationships. They describe patterns to look for; they are not visual proof or templates to copy. Project-specific approved examples outrank them.

## Hierarchy: equal emphasis versus dominant message

Constant brief: announce a workshop with a title, date, CTA, and logo.

Weak relationship:

- title, date, CTA, and logo use similar size and contrast;
- four separate groups compete;
- reduced-size viewing preserves no obvious entry point.

Stronger relationship:

- the workshop title is one P0 group;
- date is nearby P1 support;
- CTA follows after a larger intergroup gap;
- logo occupies a quiet attribution region;
- the same copy remains exact.

Causal lesson: reducing secondary emphasis can improve hierarchy more effectively than adding decoration to the headline.

## Grouping: even distribution versus semantic proximity

Constant content: headline, two-line explanation, CTA, and legal footer.

Weak relationship:

- every vertical gap is equal;
- CTA appears closer in meaning to the legal footer than to the explanation it concludes.

Stronger relationship:

- headline and explanation use a small intragroup gap;
- CTA follows with a deliberate but related gap;
- legal footer is separated by the largest gap and lower contrast.

Causal lesson: spacing communicates relationship; equal distances can erase information architecture.

## Text on imagery: coordinate fit versus semantic fit

Constant image: a portrait looking left with quiet space on the left.

Weak relationship:

- copy is placed over the face because that area fits the bounding box;
- white shadowed type crosses mixed skin and background values;
- the face and headline compete.

Stronger relationship:

- face, hair, and gaze space are treated as protected;
- copy occupies the quiet left field and aligns to a meaningful image edge;
- crop and text form one reading path;
- no effect is needed for basic readability.

Causal lesson: semantic and perceptual image structure matter more than unused coordinates.

## Typography: shrink-to-fit versus structural response

Constant locked copy: a long campaign statement.

Weak relationship:

- every line is reduced until the full sentence fits one narrow box;
- the primary message becomes unreadable at mobile size.

Stronger relationship:

- the line breaks preserve phrases;
- the copy field becomes wider or the composition changes archetype;
- optional P3 detail moves to a separate utility group;
- the primary role remains legible.

Causal lesson: type size is part of the viewing-context contract; solve excess content through hierarchy and layout before shrinking.

## Brand: adjective literalism versus observable grammar

Constant direction: “premium and technical.”

Weak relationship:

- black, gold, neon blue, glass cards, and random grid labels appear from category clichés;
- no supplied Brand evidence supports them.

Stronger relationship:

- premium becomes controlled space, limited roles, precise alignment, and restrained detail;
- technical becomes repeatable measurement, systematic labels, and a defined grid;
- exact Brand tokens and approved imagery determine color and type.

Causal lesson: adjectives need observable behavior and Brand evidence, not automatic style recipes.

## Revision: global rebuild versus localized repair

Constant render: a strong composition with one orphaned final headline word.

Weak response:

- replaces the typeface, crop, palette, and layout;
- loses already successful relationships.

Stronger response:

- adjusts the text box width or one intentional line break;
- rerenders and checks that hierarchy and adjacent spacing remain stable.

Causal lesson: local defects should normally receive local fixes.

## Adaptation: uniform scale versus recomposition

Constant campaign: portrait social post adapted to landscape thumbnail.

Weak relationship:

- scales the entire portrait composition down;
- face becomes small, headline wraps poorly, and edge-safe content collides with the crop.

Stronger relationship:

- preserves message, subject, and type-role hierarchy;
- chooses a closer crop, shorter line shape, and new group order;
- removes optional P3 detail;
- verifies the new canvas independently.

Causal lesson: cross-format consistency comes from preserved relationships, not identical coordinates.
